I develop the foundations of trustworthy AI for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, using tools from operations research, Bayesian statistics, and machine learning. My main application is healthcare, where decision systems must learn from imperfect records, data from other populations, and AI predictions, and where their failures are costly.

My work rests on two ideas. First, imperfect information should be *borrowed, not trusted*: a learning system should judge how much each source deserves as evidence accumulates. Second, trustworthiness is a matter of *risk, not averages*: a decision system must be judged by how badly it can fail, not only by how it performs on average. Recent work warm-starts online learning from confounded observational data while learning how far to trust it, and shows how tempering the posterior controls the risk of Thompson Sampling. I am now applying these ideas to organ transplantation, mobile health, and personalized dosing.

I teach management science, spreadsheet modeling and simulation, and a graduate course I designed on AI-enhanced optimization, in which students learn to use generative AI for modeling while critically verifying its output. In 2025–26 I served as Academic Director of the B.S. program in Business Analytics and Information Management.

I hold a Ph.D. in Industrial Engineering from Purdue University and a B.Tech. in Mechanical Engineering from the Indian Institute of Technology, Patna. Before joining the Daniels School, I was a postdoctoral research associate in the Department of Statistics at Texas A&M University. During my graduate studies, I was a Givens Associate at Argonne National Laboratory.

**Research interests:** sequential decision-making, bandits and reinforcement learning, data-driven and stochastic optimization, Bayesian statistics, healthcare operations, and precision medicine.
